# AGREE II Per-Item Scoring Criteria
## For embedding in AI extraction prompt — MSK CPG Gender Review Tool

**Source:** AGREE II Users' Manual and 23-item Instrument (AGREE Next Steps Consortium, 2009)
**URL:** https://www.agreetrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/AGREE_II_Users_Manual_and_23-item_Instrument_ENGLISH.pdf

---

## General Scoring Principles

All 23 items are rated on a 7-point scale:
- **1 (Strongly Disagree):** No relevant information present, or concept very poorly reported
- **2–3:** Minimal reporting; very few criteria met
- **4 (Neither Agree nor Disagree):** Partial reporting; some but not most criteria met
- **5–6:** Substantial reporting; most criteria met but not all
- **7 (Strongly Agree):** Exceptional reporting; all criteria fully met and considerations addressed

The score increases as more criteria are met. Criteria are explicit elements that must be present. Considerations are judgment factors that inform the score within the range implied by the criteria count.

---

## DOMAIN 1: SCOPE AND PURPOSE

### Item 1: The overall objective(s) of the guideline is (are) specifically described.

**Where to look:** Introduction, scope, purpose, rationale, background, objectives sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Health intent(s) described (e.g., prevention, screening, diagnosis, treatment)
- Expected benefit or outcome specified
- Target (patient population or society) identified

**Considerations:**
- Is the objective well written, clear, and concise?
- Is it easy to find in the guideline?

**Score 1:** No objectives stated or extremely vague
**Score 7:** All three criteria met; objectives are specific, clearly written, easy to locate, and directly linked to expected health benefits

---

### Item 2: The health question(s) covered by the guideline is (are) specifically described.

**Where to look:** Introduction, scope, purpose, rationale, background, questions sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Target population specified
- Intervention(s) or exposure(s) described
- Comparisons stated (if appropriate)
- Outcome(s) described
- Health care setting or context described

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is there enough information for anyone to understand the populations and contexts profiled?

**Score 1:** No health questions described
**Score 7:** All five criteria met; questions are detailed, specific, and clearly presented

---

### Item 3: The population to whom the guideline is meant to apply is specifically described.

**Where to look:** Introduction, scope, purpose, patient population, target population sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Target population, sex/gender, and age range described
- Clinical condition described (if relevant)
- Severity or stage of disease described (if relevant)
- Comorbidities described (if relevant)
- Excluded populations described (if relevant)

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is the population information specific enough so that the correct individuals would receive the recommended action?

**Score 1:** No population described
**Score 7:** All relevant criteria met; population is precisely and comprehensively described including exclusions

---

## DOMAIN 2: STAKEHOLDER INVOLVEMENT

### Item 4: The guideline development group includes individuals from all relevant professional groups.

**Where to look:** Introduction, acknowledgements, appendices, methods, guideline panel member list.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
For each member of the guideline development group, the following is provided:
- Name
- Discipline or content expertise (e.g., neurosurgeon, methodologist)
- Institution
- Geographical location
- Description of the member's role in the guideline development group

**Considerations:**
- Is the information clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are the members an appropriate match for the topic and scope?
- Is there at least one methodology expert (e.g., systematic review expert, epidemiologist, statistician)?

**Score 1:** No development group information provided
**Score 7:** All five criteria met for all members; multidisciplinary group clearly described with roles and expertise

---

### Item 5: The views and preferences of the target population (patients, public, etc.) have been sought.

**Where to look:** Scope, methods, guideline panel member list, external review, target population perspectives sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Statement of type of strategy used to capture patients'/public's views (e.g., participation in development group, literature review of values and preferences)
- Methods by which preferences and views were sought described (e.g., surveys, focus groups, evidence from literature)
- Outcomes or information gathered from patient/public described
- Description of how information gathered was used to inform guideline development or recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?

**Score 1:** No evidence of patient/public involvement or perspective-seeking
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; patient/public involvement is explicitly described at multiple stages

---

### Item 6: The target users of the guideline are clearly defined.

**Where to look:** Introduction, target user, intended user sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Clear description of intended guideline audience (e.g., specialists, GPs, patients, administrators)
- Description of how the guideline may be used by its target audience (e.g., to inform clinical decisions, policy, standards of care)

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are the target users appropriate for the scope of the guideline?

**Score 1:** No target users identified
**Score 7:** Both criteria met; target users are explicitly named, clearly described, and appropriate to the scope

---

## DOMAIN 3: RIGOUR OF DEVELOPMENT

### Item 7: Systematic methods were used to search for evidence.

**Where to look:** Methods, literature search strategy, appendices.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Named electronic database(s) or evidence source(s) where search was performed
- Time periods searched
- Search terms used
- Full search strategy included (e.g., in appendix)

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is the search relevant and appropriate to answer the health question?
- Is there enough information for anyone to replicate the search?

**Score 1:** No description of search strategy
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; search is comprehensive, replicable, and explicitly documented

---

### Item 8: The criteria for selecting the evidence are clearly described.

**Where to look:** Methods, literature search, inclusion/exclusion criteria, appendices.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Description of inclusion criteria, including:
  - Target population characteristics
  - Study design
  - Comparisons (if relevant)
  - Outcomes
  - Language (if relevant)
  - Context (if relevant)
- Description of exclusion criteria (if relevant)

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is there a rationale given for chosen inclusion/exclusion criteria?
- Do criteria align with the health question(s)?
- Are there reasons to believe relevant literature may not have been considered?

**Score 1:** No inclusion/exclusion criteria described
**Score 7:** All inclusion and exclusion criteria explicitly described with rationale; criteria are comprehensive and aligned with the health question

---

### Item 9: The strengths and limitations of the body of evidence are clearly described.

**Where to look:** Methods, results, discussion, evidence tables sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Descriptions of how the body of evidence was evaluated for bias
- Descriptions of how it was interpreted by the guideline development group
- Aspects addressed should include as many of the following as relevant:
  - Study design(s) in body of evidence
  - Study methodology limitations (sampling, blinding, allocation, analytical methods)
  - Appropriateness of primary and secondary outcomes considered
  - Consistency of results across studies
  - Direction of results across studies
  - Magnitude of benefit versus harm
  - Applicability to practice context

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are descriptions appropriate, neutral, and unbiased?

**Score 1:** No description of evidence strengths or limitations
**Score 7:** Comprehensive, explicit, unbiased description of evidence quality across all relevant aspects

---

### Item 10: The methods for formulating the recommendations are clearly described.

**Where to look:** Methods, guideline development process sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Description of the recommendation development process (e.g., steps in modified Delphi, voting procedures)
- Outcomes of the recommendation development process (e.g., extent of consensus reached, outcome of voting)
- Description of how the process influenced the recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Was a formal process used?
- Were the methods appropriate?

**Score 1:** No description of how recommendations were formulated
**Score 7:** All three criteria met; formal, explicit, and transparent process described with outcomes

---

### Item 11: The health benefits, side effects, and risks have been considered in formulating the recommendations.

**Where to look:** Methods, interpretation, discussion, recommendations sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Supporting data and report of benefits
- Supporting data and report of harms, side effects, or risks
- Reporting of the balance or trade-off between benefits and harms
- Recommendations reflect considerations of both benefits and harms

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is the discussion an integral part of the guideline development process rather than an afterthought?
- Has the development group considered benefits and harms equally?

**Score 1:** No consideration of benefits, harms, or risks
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; benefits and harms explicitly balanced and reflected in recommendations

---

### Item 12: There is an explicit link between the recommendations and the supporting evidence.

**Where to look:** Recommendations, key evidence sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Guideline describes how the development group linked and used evidence to inform recommendations
- Each recommendation is linked to a key evidence description, paragraph, or reference list
- Recommendations linked to evidence summaries or evidence tables in results section

**Considerations:**
- Is there congruency between evidence and recommendations?
- Is the link easy to find?
- When evidence is lacking or recommendation is primarily consensus-based, is this clearly stated?

**Score 1:** No link between recommendations and evidence
**Score 7:** All three criteria met; every recommendation is explicitly traceable to supporting evidence

---

### Item 13: The guideline has been externally reviewed by experts prior to its publication.

**Where to look:** Methods, results, interpretation, acknowledgements sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Purpose and intent of the external review described
- Methods used to undertake the external review described
- Description of the external reviewers (number, type, affiliations)
- Outcomes or information gathered from the external review described
- Description of how information gathered was used to inform guideline development or recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are the external reviewers relevant and appropriate to the scope?
- Was there a rationale for choosing the reviewers?
- How was information from the external review used?

**Score 1:** No mention of external review
**Score 7:** All five criteria met; external review process comprehensively documented

---

### Item 14: A procedure for updating the guideline is provided.

**Where to look:** Introduction, methods, guideline update, closing sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- A statement that the guideline will be updated
- Explicit time interval or explicit criteria to guide decisions about when an update will occur
- Methodology for the updating procedure is reported

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is there enough information to know when an update will occur or what criteria would trigger an update?

**Score 1:** No mention of an updating procedure
**Score 7:** All three criteria met; specific timeframe and methodology for updating explicitly stated

---

## DOMAIN 4: CLARITY OF PRESENTATION

### Item 15: The recommendations are specific and unambiguous.

**Where to look:** Recommendations, executive summary sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Statement of the recommended action
- Identification of the intent or purpose of the recommended action
- Identification of the relevant population
- Caveats or qualifying statements included (if relevant)

**Considerations:**
- In the event of multiple recommendations, is there clarity regarding to whom each applies?
- If there is uncertainty in the evidence, is that uncertainty explicitly reflected in the recommendations?

**Score 1:** Recommendations are vague or absent
**Score 7:** All criteria met; recommendations are precise, actionable, and include caveats where appropriate

---

### Item 16: The different options for management of the condition or health issue are clearly presented.

**Where to look:** Executive summary, recommendations, discussion, treatment options sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Description of options
- Description of population or clinical situation most appropriate to each option

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is this a guideline broad or narrow in scope? (This item is more relevant to broad guidelines covering management of a condition rather than a narrow intervention-specific guideline)

**Score 1:** No management options presented
**Score 7:** Both criteria met; all relevant options are clearly described with appropriate population/situation guidance

---

### Item 17: Key recommendations are easily identifiable.

**Where to look:** Executive summary, conclusions, recommendations sections; separate quick reference guides.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Key recommendations presented in a summarised box, typed in bold, underlined, or as flow charts or algorithms
- Specific recommendations grouped together in one section

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are key recommendations appropriately selected and do they reflect the key messages?
- Are specific recommendations grouped near the summary of key evidence?

**Score 1:** Key recommendations are not identifiable or distinguishable from the body text
**Score 7:** Both criteria met; key recommendations are visually distinct, grouped, and easy to locate

---

## DOMAIN 5: APPLICABILITY

### Item 18: The guideline describes facilitators and barriers to its application.

**Where to look:** Dissemination/implementation sections, quality indicators, barriers sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Identification of the types of facilitators and barriers considered
- Methods by which information on facilitators and barriers was sought (e.g., stakeholder feedback, pilot testing)
- Information or description of the types of facilitators and barriers that emerged
- Description of how this information influenced guideline development or recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Does the guideline suggest specific strategies for overcoming barriers?

**Score 1:** No mention of facilitators or barriers
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; facilitators and barriers explicitly identified and addressed in recommendations

---

### Item 19: The guideline provides advice and/or tools on how the recommendations can be put into practice.

**Where to look:** Implementation, tools, resources, appendices sections; accompanying materials.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- An implementation section in the guideline
- Tools and resources to facilitate application present, such as:
  - Guideline summary documents
  - Links to checklists or algorithms
  - Links to how-to manuals
  - Solutions linked to barrier analysis
  - Tools to capitalise on guideline facilitators
  - Outcome of pilot test and lessons learned
- Directions on how users can access tools and resources

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is there information about the development and validation of implementation tools?

**Score 1:** No implementation tools or advice provided
**Score 7:** All criteria met; comprehensive implementation support provided and accessible

---

### Item 20: The potential resource implications of applying the recommendations have been considered.

**Where to look:** Methods, cost utility, cost effectiveness, acquisition costs, budget implications sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Identification of the types of cost information considered (e.g., economic evaluations, drug acquisition costs)
- Methods by which cost information was sought (e.g., health economist on panel, health technology assessments)
- Information or description of cost findings
- Description of how cost information was used to inform guideline development or recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Were appropriate experts involved in finding and analysing cost information?

**Score 1:** No consideration of resource or cost implications
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; resource implications comprehensively analysed and explicitly integrated into recommendations

---

### Item 21: The guideline presents monitoring and/or auditing criteria.

**Where to look:** Recommendations, quality indicators, monitoring, audit sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Criteria for monitoring or auditing the application of recommendations are provided
- Criteria are clearly derived from key recommendations
- Type of measure specified (process, behavioural, clinical, or outcome measure)

**Considerations:**
- Is the description clear and concise?
- Is it easy to find?
- Are the criteria measurable and feasible?

**Score 1:** No monitoring or auditing criteria provided
**Score 7:** All criteria met; explicit, measurable monitoring criteria directly linked to key recommendations

---

## DOMAIN 6: EDITORIAL INDEPENDENCE

### Item 22: The views of the funding body have not influenced the content of the guideline.

**Where to look:** Preface, methods, acknowledgements, funding/sponsorship sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- Name of funding body is provided
- An explicit statement that the views or interests of the funding body have not influenced the final recommendations

**Considerations:**
- Is the statement credible?
- Is there congruency between the stated independence and the content of the guideline?

**Score 1:** No mention of funding source or independence
**Score 7:** Both criteria met; funding source named and explicit independence statement provided

---

### Item 23: Competing interests of members of the guideline development group have been recorded and addressed.

**Where to look:** Preface, methods, acknowledgements, appendices, conflict of interest sections.

**Criteria (score increases with each criterion met):**
- A statement that interests of development group members have been sought
- Description of the types of interests that were sought (e.g., financial, non-financial)
- Methods by which interests were sought and recorded described
- Description of how the interests identified were addressed

**Considerations:**
- Is the statement clear and credible?
- Is it easy to find?
- Is there congruency between stated competing interests and the content and recommendations of the guideline?

**Score 1:** No mention of competing interests
**Score 7:** All four criteria met; interests comprehensively sought, recorded, and addressed with explicit process described

## Domain Score Calculation (Single Appraiser)

Formula: **(Sum of item scores − Minimum possible) ÷ (Maximum possible − Minimum possible) × 100**

| Domain | Items | N items | Min | Max |
|--------|-------|---------|-----|-----|
| Domain 1: Scope and Purpose | 1–3 | 3 | 3 | 21 |
| Domain 2: Stakeholder Involvement | 4–6 | 3 | 3 | 21 |
| Domain 3: Rigour of Development | 7–14 | 8 | 8 | 56 |
| Domain 4: Clarity of Presentation | 15–17 | 3 | 3 | 21 |
| Domain 5: Applicability | 18–21 | 4 | 4 | 28 |
| Domain 6: Editorial Independence | 22–23 | 2 | 2 | 14 |

---

## Overall Quality Rating (per parent review protocol)

- **High quality:** 5 or more domains score above 60%
- **Average quality:** 3 or 4 domains score above 60%
- **Low quality:** 2 or fewer domains score above 60%
